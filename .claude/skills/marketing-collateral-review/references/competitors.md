## Patterns across the market

**What most do**
- White or pale ground with one saturated brand hue: green (Brighte, Bridgit accent), orange (humm), purple (Zip, Payright), blue (Latitude, Plenti navy), red/coral (Harmoney, Renosell). Friendly grotesk typefaces, often custom (Nuckle, Daylight, Garnett, Sharp Grotesk, Latitude Sans) or Google sans (Roboto, Montserrat, Inter, Be Vietnam Pro).
- Imagery is lifestyle photography (families, gardens, installers) plus flat icons; the pay-later-for-sellers players use before/after renovation shots.
- CTA vocabulary clusters on "Apply now", "Get a quote / Get your rate / Check your rate", "Get started", and for partners "Get accredited", "Book a call/demo", "Log in to portal".
- Merchant-distributed lenders (Brighte, Zip, humm, Payright, Latitude) publish self-serve marketing hubs: logo packs, brand and advertising guidelines, flyers, social tiles with approved copy, web badges/banners, in-store POS kits (ordered through the portal), plus an approval mailbox. Property-secured lenders and equity release (Bridgit, Funding, Household Capital) instead rely on broker accreditation, portals, BDMs, calculators and long-form guides/ebooks — no public asset library.
- Partner mechanics that shape collateral: partner-coded apply URLs (Handypay `register-get-started/<Partner>` and `brokerId=`; Latitude `merchantID=`), embeddable widgets/banners for partner sites (Latitude price-per-month widgets, Zip banners, humm widgets), partner apps for on-site quoting (Brighte, CampaignAgent), and referral fees (Household Capital, Bridgit, Wisr).

**What nobody does (in the fetched set)**
- No one publishes an open co-branding generator or lets partners drop their own logo onto templates; co-branding is asserted in words (Handypay/HIA, Elepay, Payright's landing-page template and "submit your brand assets") rather than shown. QR codes were not seen on any fetched page. No one uses a serif display face or a metallic/champagne accent. No one pairs a dark ground with warm gold; the only dark palettes are MoneyMe (dark green + lime), Wisr (navy + pastels) and Flipro's near-black blocks, and the only warm-neutral palette is Household Capital (cream, beige, amber).

**Where Propential's dark-and-champagne look sits**
- It is the only property-finance brand in the set with a dark ground, a metallic accent (#D6B15E), a serif display face (Fraunces) and mono numerals. That reads as premium/considered against a market that reads friendly-fintech (bright hue on white). Nearest neighbours for benchmarking: MoneyMe for a dark lender palette executed with consumer-friendly warmth; Wisr for dark navy with restrained pastels and illustration; Household Capital for warm-neutral trust cues aimed at homeowners with equity. Design risk to check against Brighte/Zip/Payright: those hubs show how much rate/fee copy and partner instruction text ends up on flyers and tiles — Propential's darker, lower-contrast surfaces (#111310 with muted text #A7A399) will need care for small print and print reproduction.
- Three strongest design references for the designer: (1) Brighte marketing hub — best-structured partner asset set (per-product flyer + social tiles + approved copy + vendor badge + approval rule); (2) Payright resources page — the only one exposing fonts/colours/logo/landing-page template as a public kit; (3) MoneyMe renovation page — proof a dark palette can carry a consumer renovation loan.
